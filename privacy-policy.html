2. Music Playback

Spotify

The App does not connect to your Spotify account. When Spotify is your chosen music source, the App shows a button that opens the Spotify app, or Spotify's App Store page if it isn't installed. The App does not sign in to Spotify, does not read or control what Spotify plays, and receives no information from Spotify. To label that button, the App checks on your device whether Spotify is installed; the result is not sent anywhere. Once Spotify is open, your use of it is governed by Spotify's Privacy Policy.

Apple Music

If you choose to connect Apple Music, the App uses Apple's MusicKit framework, with your permission, to show your library and recently played songs, search the Apple Music catalog, and play music. Playback requires an active Apple Music subscription. This information is provided by Apple and is not sent to us. Analytics records only whether connecting succeeded or why it failed (for example, permission denied or no subscription), never song titles or your listening history. Your use of Apple Music is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy. You can remove the connection in the App's Settings. The Apple Music permission itself is managed in iOS Settings.

Radio

The App's radio feature uses the Radio Browser public, community-maintained API to list internet radio stations by the country you choose. When you select a station, the audio stream is delivered directly from the broadcaster, which receives your IP address like any other internet request. We do not operate our own streaming servers and do not track which stations you listen to. Some stations stream only over unencrypted HTTP. In that case the audio is delivered without encryption. Your favorite stations are stored only on your device.

Podcasts

To find podcasts, the App uses Apple's public iTunes Search API. Your search terms and your device's region are sent to Apple to return matching shows and popular shows in your region. When you open a show, the App downloads that show's public RSS feed directly from its publisher. Episode audio is streamed directly from the publisher or its hosting provider, some of which use unencrypted HTTP. These publishers receive your IP address as with any internet request. The shows you follow are stored only on your device. We do not track which episodes you listen to.

3. Information Collected Through Analytics

We use Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics, both provided by Google LLC, to understand general App usage and to detect and fix crashes.

Analytics may process information such as: app opens and sessions, screens or features viewed, general feature interactions, session duration, app version, operating-system information, device model, language, approximate regional information (derived by Google from your IP address), an automatically generated app-instance identifier, and Apple's Identifier for Vendor (IDFV) where applicable.

The events the App itself records are limited to: which introduction screens were viewed and whether the location prompt was answered; which tabs were selected; that navigation started, with a coarse trip-distance range and whether alternative routes were available; whether connecting Apple Music succeeded, with a fixed error category if it failed; whether the notification permission prompt was shown and answered, and where it was shown; that a notification was opened, with its type (for example "home" or "podcast"); that a destination was chosen on the map by long-pressing or by tapping a place name (not the place itself); and that the App Store rating request was triggered, with what triggered it, or that the rating card was tapped. Whether you actually leave a rating is not visible to us.

Crashlytics may process: stack traces, device model, iOS version, and app version at the time of a crash.

We do not intentionally send the following to Google Analytics for Firebase or Crashlytics: your name, email address, phone number, address book contents, precise GPS coordinates, your music listening history, or call contents.

This data is pseudonymous: it is associated with a random app-instance identifier, not with your name, email address, or phone number. Where applicable law (such as GDPR) requires a legal basis for this processing, we rely on legitimate interest — understanding aggregate usage and fixing crashes to keep the App reliable — rather than data that identifies you individually. The App does not currently offer an in-app control to disable Analytics; you may still limit data collection generally through your iOS device settings (e.g., disabling "Share iPhone Analytics" in Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements).

4. Identifiers

App-Instance Identifier

Firebase generates an identifier associated with a particular installation of the App, used to calculate metrics such as active users and sessions. It is not created from your name, email, or phone number.

Identifier for Vendor (IDFV)

Firebase may use Apple's IDFV, an identifier shared across apps from the same developer on your device, for analytics purposes. We do not use IDFV to identify you by name or to track you across apps from unrelated developers.

Identifier for Advertisers (IDFA)

The App does not use Apple's IDFA. We do not use IDFA for advertising, attribution, profiling, or cross-app tracking of any kind.

Some radio stations and podcast audio files are only available over unencrypted HTTP; that audio is played directly from the broadcaster or publisher. We do not operate our own servers, so no personal data is stored on infrastructure we control. iOS application sandboxing and permission controls provide additional protection. No method of electronic storage or transmission is completely secure, but we follow industry best practices.
